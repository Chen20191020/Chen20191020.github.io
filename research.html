Research

My research focuses on nonparametric time series, stochastic processes, asymptotic theory, and statistical inference for dependent data.

I study locally stationary processes whose distributional and dependence structures evolve over time. A central goal of my work is to develop estimators that remain uniformly accurate over the full time domain and inference procedures that are valid under temporal dependence.

My current research combines orthonormal-basis sieve estimation, physical dependence measures, uniform convergence analysis, and multiplier bootstrap methods. I am particularly interested in explicit convergence rates, increasing-lag regimes, simultaneous confidence bands, and statistically rigorous learning procedures for nonstationary data.

Selected Research Projects

  • Simultaneous Inference for Locally Stationary Time Series

    Sieve estimation · Uniform convergence · Dependent data · Multiplier bootstrap

    Sept. 2025 – Present
    • Developed sieve estimators for time-varying trend, autocovariance, and autocorrelation using orthonormal basis expansions and ordinary least squares.
    • Established uniform consistency and explicit convergence rates under polynomially decaying physical dependence, including admissible regimes in which the lag increases with the sample size.
    • Developing Gaussian multiplier-bootstrap simultaneous confidence bands for time-varying dependence over the full time domain, including boundary regions.

  • Structural Classification of Locally Stationary Time Series

    Second-order structure · Autoregressive approximation · Classification theory

    Nov. 2024 – Apr. 2026
    • Developed a classification framework for locally stationary time series based on time-varying second-order structure and autoregressive approximation.
    • Established conditions under which the misclassification error converges to zero, without requiring the training sample size to increase.
    • Verified the finite-sample theory through simulations and real data, achieving up to 94.7% test accuracy and outperforming statistical and deep-learning benchmarks.

    Journal articlearXivCode

  • Time-Frequency Methods for Nonstationary Signals

    Pseudo Wigner–Ville distributions · Wavelet packet transforms · Statistical representation

    Apr. 2025 – June 2025
    • Studied Pseudo Wigner–Ville distributions and wavelet packet transforms as alternative representations of localized time-frequency structure in nonstationary signals.
    • Compared their statistical resolution, predictive performance, and computational cost in financial forecasting and biomedical classification using random forests and convolutional neural networks.
    • Achieved 74.7% mean directional accuracy under the best financial-model configuration and 81.3% biomedical classification accuracy, while identifying a substantial accuracy-runtime tradeoff between the two representations.
