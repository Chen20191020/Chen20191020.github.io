Research

My research develops statistical and deep learning methods for sequential data, especially when signals are nonstationary and their temporal dependence evolves over time.

I study how to construct informative representations of complex temporal data, establish reliable statistical guarantees under dependence, and quantify uncertainty over an entire time domain. My work combines interpretable time-series modeling, time-frequency feature extraction, machine learning, and bootstrap-based inference, with applications to biomedical signals and financial data.

The broader goal is to create methods that are theoretically reliable, computationally scalable, and useful for real-world prediction and decision making.

Selected Research Projects

  • Structural Classification of Locally Stationary Time Series

    Time series · Machine learning · Biomedical signals

    Nov. 2024 – Apr. 2026
    • Developed an interpretable classification framework for sequential data using features derived from time-varying second-order dependence.
    • Estimated time-varying autoregressive structure through sieve-based approximation and summarized local versus global temporal variation for classification.
    • Evaluated the method on brain signal data and benchmarked it against 10 competing approaches, including convolution methods, shapelet methods, random forests, and recurrent neural networks.
    • Achieved up to 94.7% test accuracy on abnormal-versus-normal brain signal classification, outperforming competitive statistical and deep learning baselines.

  • Time-Frequency Analysis for Nonstationary Signals

    PWVD · Wavelet packet transforms · Machine learning · Deep learning

    Apr. 2025 – June 2025
    • Converted raw financial price and brain signals into time-frequency features that capture how signal patterns change over time and frequency, using pseudo-Wigner–Ville distributions (PWVD) and wavelet packet transforms (WPT).
    • Built and evaluated machine-learning models to predict future price direction across 26 financial assets and to classify brain signals as normal or abnormal.
    • Achieved 74.7% average price-direction accuracy under the best financial-model setting and 81.3% brain signal classification accuracy.
    • Found that WPT was approximately 33 times faster than PWVD, while PWVD provided slightly higher average prediction accuracy.
